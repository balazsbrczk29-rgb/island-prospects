# Playbook: technology and security (IT, POS, web, alarms and CCTV, armoured transport, patrol)

## 1. The business and the buyer
IT and point-of-sale providers, web studios, alarm and camera installers, security patrol firms, cash-in-transit.
Buyers: business owners, store and restaurant managers, property managers, banks, government. They fear:
downtime, theft, a vendor who disappears after installation. They choose the provider who looks technically
sharp, reliable and present, with proof.

## 2. What wins the owner
"Your company finally looks as modern and dependable as the systems you install." Many have dated sites; for
web studios, a dated site is an existential problem (BVG is also a competitor: pitch with care).

## 3. Big ideas
- **The system, explained.** An object or system study: the real hardware (POS terminal, camera, alarm panel,
  network rack, armoured van) studio-lit in 3D or photography, and scroll reveals what each part does for the
  client. (ModelStage, ScrollStage)
- **Your site, protected.** For security: an interactive plan of a typical property (shop, home, warehouse)
  where the visitor places doors and windows and sees what coverage looks like; ends in a site survey request.
  It must look like a precise architectural plan, never a toy.
- **24 hours.** For patrol and monitoring: the page moves through a full day and night of protection, light
  changing from day to night, each hour with what the team actually does (sourced services only).
- **The product UI.** For POS and software: real product screens (from the vendor's public material where
  allowed) shown in context on a real counter; for web studios, their own portfolio as the hero.

## 4. The page
The promise (big idea), services or systems (real), how it works (survey, install, support), proof (clients,
certifications, years: sourced only), support and response, request a survey or quote, contact.

## 5. Imagery and 3D
Their own install photos; Lovable for the trade (an officer's back at a gate at dusk, a camera on a pastel
Nassau wall, hands at a terminal), labelled illustrative. ModelStage for hardware. Never hacker clichés,
padlock icons, glowing circuit boards or matrix rain.

## 6. Interactions
The coverage planner; a quote or survey request (property type, size, system, island: Enquiry); a support or
emergency contact that is unmistakable.

## 7. Motion
Crisp and precise: fast, exact responses, lines that draw like technical diagrams, calm otherwise.

## 8. Type and colour
A modern grotesk with a mono for technical details; colour from their brand or a confident single accent on
light, clean grounds; one dark section for night or monitoring if the idea calls for it.

## 9. Voice
Direct, reassuring, concrete. Tone example, write your own: "If the alarm goes at 3 am, someone answers. Here
is who."

## 10. Conversion
Primary: request a site survey or quote. Secondary: call; for clients, a clear support line.

## 11. SEO
`ProfessionalService`, `ComputerStore`, `SecurityService` (as `LocalBusiness` subtype text), `areaServed`.
Queries: "POS systems Bahamas", "security company Nassau", "alarm installation Nassau".

## 12. References
verkada.com (security hardware as product), linear.app (precision and craft), stripe.com (explaining systems
beautifully), anduril.com (serious, cinematic hardware presentation).

## 13. Traps
Padlock and shield icons; hacker or matrix imagery; fearmongering; invented clients or response times;
paperwork pages of codes and tables.

## 14. Done looks like
A store owner understands in five seconds what they get and that this firm is serious, and requests a survey
in two taps.
