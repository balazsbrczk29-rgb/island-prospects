# Playbook: marina, yacht club, marina hotel

## 1. The business and the buyer
Bahamian marinas (Abaco, Exuma, Grand Bahama, Bimini, Eleuthera) sell slips, fuel, shore power, customs
clearance and a sense of arrival. The buyer is a yacht or sportfish owner, often from Florida or the US East
Coast, high net worth, planning a passage; or their captain checking depth, length and fuel. They compare you
with every other harbour on the route and with Florida marinas they know. They fear: running aground in the
approach, no slip on arrival, a marina that feels run-down. They choose a place that feels calm, competent
and quietly exclusive, and that answers the captain's questions in seconds.

**Variant: private club and residences.** Some marinas (Bell Channel) are owners' condominium clubs with a private
dock, not public marinas. Then the buyers are owners, prospective buyers or renters of units, and visiting boaters
asking whether a slip is ever available. Route them early ("Who is asking?": an owner, a prospective buyer, a
visiting boat) and sell the place to live, not transient slips.

## 2. What wins the owner
"Your harbour finally looks like the place yacht owners talk about, and a captain can check everything they
need from the cockpit on a phone." Most marina sites here are dated brochures or boating-guide pages.

## 3. Big ideas (pick one, or invent a truer one)
- **The arrival.** A ScrollStage with the Ocean recipe at dusk or first light: scrolling carries the visitor
  across open water toward the harbour; chapter captions name the real waypoints (the channel, the markers, the
  island). At the end the scene dissolves into a real photograph of the docks. If no real dock photo exists (often), end on an
  excellent Commons photo of the real bay or inlet, or on a still rendered from the scene. (Ocean, ScrollStage, PhotoDepth)
- **Will she fit?** A precise, elegant slip checker as the hero: enter length, beam and draft; the page shows
  the marina's sourced limits and answers yes, ask, or no, with a calm visual of the hull against the slip.
  Only with sourced numbers; otherwise "Ask the dockmaster" states clearly what to ask. (Enquiry for the request)
- **The harbour at four hours.** One real photograph or Ocean scene that shifts from morning to blue hour as you
  scroll, with what happens at each hour (fuel dock opens, the bar, the quiet night). (Ocean elevation by progress, PhotoDepth)
- **Quiet luxury.** Almost no interface: one full-bleed image per screen, a single sentence each, a calling-card
  contact screen. Right for the most exclusive clubs.
- **Specification as beauty.** For technical marinas: a sharp spec sheet for captains (LOA, beam, draft at MLW,
  power, fuel, VHF channel, port of entry) set like a yacht builder's data page, beside one huge photograph.

## 4. The page (adapt the order to the story)
Arrival (the big idea), what the harbour is (one or two true sentences), the docks and services (specs a captain
trusts), the island and what is ashore (dining, beaches, town, only real places), getting here (coordinates only
if sourced, approach notes only if sourced, nearest airport and customs), request a slip, contact and VHF.

## 5. Imagery and 3D
Their own dock photos first; Commons photos of the real harbour, island or lighthouse; Lovable for atmosphere
(yacht sterns at slips at blue hour, cleats and lines, an aerial of calm water). The Ocean recipe is made for
this type: golden hour (elevation 2-5) or blue hour (elevation -1). Never generate their own docks or boats.

## 6. Interactions
The slip request flow (dates, length, beam, draft, power needed: Enquiry); the slip fit check (sourced limits);
a harbour map with real points (fuel dock, office, restaurant) if a real layout is published.

## 7. Motion
Slow and maritime: long eases, gentle camera drift like a boat at anchor, nothing that snaps. Captions fade like
film subtitles.

## 8. Type and colour
Refined serif display or a precise grotesk with generous tracking at small sizes. Colour from the water and the
hulls: deep navy or harbour teal fields, white and teak, brass as a rare accent. Light pages with one or two deep
sections. Never nautical clip art, rope borders or anchor icons.

## 9. Voice
Calm, specific, confident, written for a captain. Short sentences with real facts. Tone example, write your own:
"Twenty-three slips, protected on all sides. Call the dockmaster on channel 16 before you round the point."

## 10. Conversion
Primary: request a slip (WhatsApp, email or VHF and phone). Secondary: call the office. The phone number and
VHF channel, if sourced, are visible from the first screen.

## 11. SEO
`Marina` (or `LodgingBusiness` plus `Marina`), `geo` only if sourced, `amenityFeature` for fuel, power, water,
`containsPlace` for the restaurant. Queries: "<island> marina", "marina <settlement> Bahamas", "slips <island>".

## 12. References (look at two)
feadship.com (one huge image per screen, tiny confident type), wallyyachts.com (restraint, product as hero),
yachtclubdemonaco.com (quiet luxury), oysteryachts.com (craft and water photography).

## 13. Traps
Anchor icons and rope; a generic stock-like yacht that could be anywhere; unsourced depths or slip counts;
a "Key information" strip; dark navy over the whole page; burying the phone and VHF.

## 14. Done looks like
A yacht owner feels they are already arriving; a captain finds length, draft, power, fuel and the channel in
under ten seconds on a phone; the owner recognises their own harbour.
