# Playbook: trades and contractors (roofing, glass, pools, upholstery, marine covers, building, paving)

## 1. The business and the buyer
Bahamian trades rebuild after hurricanes, protect homes from sun, salt and storms, and fit out resorts. Buyers:
homeowners (often with insurance claims), property and facility managers, resort engineering teams, developers,
architects. They fear: shoddy work that fails in the next storm, contractors who never call back, surprise costs.
They choose the firm that visibly knows its craft, shows real projects, and makes a quote request easy.

## 2. What wins the owner
"For the first time, people can see how good your work actually is, and request a quote from their phone."
Most trades here have no site, a Facebook page, or an old site with tiny photos.

## 3. Big ideas
- **The anatomy of the work.** An object study: the product built up layer by layer as you scroll, on a
  realistic Bahamian building (roof deck, insulation, membrane, coating; glass, frame, seal; pool shell, tile,
  coping). Callouts name each real layer or material they use. (ScrollStage + ModelStage, procedural or GLB)
- **Before the storm, after the storm.** A comparison of the same kind of building: exposed, then protected by
  their work, with the storm passing over (ScrollVideo or PhotoDepth with weather), ending in a quote request.
  Only real before and after photos are presented as their projects; generated images are illustrative.
- **The project ledger, made beautiful.** For firms with an impressive client list: each real project as a
  full-screen photograph with location, scope and year (sourced only). (HorizontalTrack)
- **Craft close-up.** For upholstery, covers and glass: macro photography of the material and the hands at work,
  very calm and tactile; the page feels like a craftsman's studio.
- **See it on your house.** An interaction where the visitor picks a finish or colour (roof membrane, glass tint,
  fabric) on a photographed house or object.

## 4. The page
The craft (big idea), what they do (their real services, as a clear list, not icon cards), proof (real projects,
clients, certifications only if sourced), how it works (survey, quote, schedule, warranty only if sourced), the
area they serve, quote request, contact.

## 5. Imagery and 3D
Their project photos first, presented large. Lovable photoreal for the trade itself (imagery.md shot list for
trades: the finished result on a real-looking Bahamian building, cross-sections, gloved hands at the critical
step, after rain), labelled illustrative. ModelStage for the anatomy idea, warm studio light.

## 6. Interactions
Quote request (property type, island, service, size, photos to send later: Enquiry); finish or colour picker;
the layer explorer.

## 7. Motion
Precise and engineered: things assemble with weight, layers settle, nothing floats. One movement at a time.

## 8. Type and colour
A strong grotesk or technical sans with a refined text face. Colour from materials: limestone white, concrete,
steel, safety yellow or the trade's real brand colour as a single accent. Light pages. Never orange-red unless
it is their brand.

## 9. Voice
Plain, competent, specific, like a foreman who knows his job. Tone example, write your own: "Every roof starts
with the deck. We check it before we sell you anything."

## 10. Conversion
Primary: request a quote or site visit. Secondary: call. WhatsApp with photos of the problem is ideal if sourced.

## 11. SEO
The right subtype: `RoofingContractor`, `HomeAndConstructionBusiness`, `GeneralContractor`; `areaServed` islands.
Queries: "roofing Nassau", "<trade> <island> Bahamas", "hurricane <product> Bahamas".

## 12. References
tesla.com/solarroof (the product on a real house, layer by layer), rivian.com (object storytelling),
apple.com product pages (one object, perfect light), fosterandpartners.com (projects as photography).

## 13. Traps
Drawn houses or toy 3D; icon grids of services; fake project photos; unsourced warranties or years; burying the
quote button; covering a builder's site in gimmicks (CBS: tape and handwriting).

## 14. Done looks like
A facility manager sees the craft is serious in five seconds, finds proof of real projects, and requests a
quote on the phone in under a minute.
