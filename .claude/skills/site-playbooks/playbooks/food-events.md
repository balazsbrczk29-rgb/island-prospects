# Playbook: food, bakeries, rum cake, catering, restaurants, weddings and events

## 1. The business and the buyer
Bahamian rum cake makers, bakeries, caterers, restaurants and bars, wedding planners and event venues. Buyers:
cruise visitors buying gifts, locals ordering for occasions, couples planning a destination wedding from abroad,
event organisers. They fear: it will not taste or look like the photo, the order will not arrive in time, the
wedding will be chaotic. They choose what looks irresistible and feels personally cared for.

## 2. What wins the owner
"Your food (or your weddings) finally look as good online as they taste and feel in person, and ordering or
enquiring is effortless." This type is also the seed of Balazs's future delivery platform: build the order
flow and `site-data.json` so it can plug in later.

## 3. Big ideas
- **The making of.** Scroll follows the craft: rum poured, batter folded, cake glazed, the box tied. Each step a
  close, warm photograph or a short film, ending at the order. (ScrollStage, ScrollVideo, PhotoDepth)
- **The object of desire.** The signature product (a rum cake, a wedding cake, a plated dish) as a studio-lit hero
  you can almost touch, turning slowly. (ModelStage with a photoreal GLB, or a perfect still with PhotoDepth)
- **The day itself.** For weddings: the wedding day as a film in chapters (arrival on the sand, the vows at
  sunset, the dinner, the last dance), with real photos from their own events where published. (ScrollStage)
- **The menu as a magazine.** For restaurants: the menu set like a beautiful printed menu, with a few dishes in
  large photographs, never a card grid.
- **The gift box.** For gifting businesses: build-your-box (choose flavours and sizes) with a live preview and
  a delivery or pickup choice (Shop or Enquiry).

## 4. The page
The craving (big idea), the signature products or packages (real), the story (real founders, real years only),
order or enquire (Shop or Enquiry), where to find them (shops, hours, cruise-port pickup if real), events,
contact. An inactive "Order online, coming soon" hook for the delivery platform where relevant.

## 5. Imagery and 3D
Their own food and event photos first, cropped big. Lovable for craft details and atmosphere (hands icing,
the table at golden hour, the venue sand), labelled illustrative; never generate their actual products as if real.
Warm, natural light; shallow depth of field.

## 6. Interactions
Build a box or an order (Shop); wedding or event enquiry (date, guests, venue type, budget band: Enquiry);
a menu filter (vegetarian, seafood) when the menu is real.

## 7. Motion
Sensual and soft: slow pours, gentle steam, warm fades. Never bouncy.

## 8. Type and colour
Expressive display (a refined serif, a Didone, or a warm script used once), a calm text face. Colour from the
food and the island: rum amber, conch pink, sugar white, palm green. Light, appetising pages.

## 9. Voice
Sensory, generous, personal. Tone example, write your own: "Soaked in rum for a full day. Wrapped by hand on
Bay Street."

## 10. Conversion
Primary: order (shop or WhatsApp) or enquire about a date. Secondary: call, visit the shop. Never "Book a Table".

## 11. SEO
`Bakery`, `FoodEstablishment`, `Restaurant` (`menu`, `servesCuisine`), `EventPlanner` or `LocalBusiness` for
weddings; `Product` and `Offer` only with real prices. Queries: "rum cake Nassau", "wedding planner Bahamas".

## 12. References
ottolenghi.co.uk (food photography as hero), laduree.com (crafted product pages), noma.dk (restraint, seasonality),
magnoliabakery.com (warmth), bluebottlecoffee.com (craft storytelling).

## 13. Traps
Cartoon food illustrations; card-grid menus; fake reviews or star ratings; invented prices; "Book a Table";
dark, moody food photos that hide the product.

## 14. Done looks like
The visitor is hungry (or emotional) within five seconds, finds what to buy or book, and orders or enquires
in two taps.
