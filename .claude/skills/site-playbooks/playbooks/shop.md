# Playbook: real online shops and retail (home centres, building supply, office supply, homeware, retail)

## 1. The business and the buyer
Retailers that already sell, often online (CBS, Kelly's, Nassau Paper, Bahamas Business Solutions). Buyers:
homeowners fixing up, contractors buying in bulk, offices restocking, Family Island customers who order and
ship by mailboat. They fear: the item is out of stock, the site is confusing, delivery to their island is
unclear. They choose the store that makes finding, ordering and getting things to their island easy.

## 2. What wins the owner
"Your shop, as easy and beautiful as the best online stores, and still yours." Their current store engine
usually looks like a generic template. The concept must be a better SHOP, never a brochure that links away
(CBS failed exactly here). This type is audit priority C: build after the A and B businesses.

## 3. Big ideas
- **The store as a magazine.** The front page is curated like Aesop or Hay: a few real products in large stills
  on one backdrop, departments as calm editorial entries, then the full catalogue. (Shop)
- **Projects, not products.** For building and home centres: shop by project ("re-roof after a storm",
  "hurricane-proof the windows", "a new kitchen"), each project assembling the real products needed into a basket.
- **To every island.** For Family Island delivery: a map-led delivery story (the order, the mailboat, the dock on
  their island), with the real mailboat options if published. Turns a pain point into the brand.
- **The counter.** For B2B suppliers: a fast trade counter: search, quantities, reorder, contractor pricing request.

## 4. The page
The curated front (big idea), departments, the catalogue with filters (Shop), product detail with real photos and
specs, cart drawer with checkout preview, delivery and pickup (stores, hours, islands), contractor or business
accounts if real, contact.

## 5. Imagery and 3D
Their real product photos (from their store, verbatim URLs), presented big on a consistent backdrop. Lovable
for lifestyle and project scenes (a finished kitchen, a delivery at a dock), labelled illustrative. A ModelStage
for one hero product only if it is iconic. Never invent products.

## 6. Interactions
Filter and search the real catalogue; add to basket; cart drawer; the project bundles; delivery-to-my-island
checker (real options only).

## 7. Motion
Fast and tactile: instant filter transitions with layout animation, a springy but restrained cart drawer.

## 8. Type and colour
A clean, characterful grotesk; generous product space; the retailer's real brand colour used with restraint;
light pages. Never discount-store red banners, never countdown timers.

## 9. Voice
Helpful and practical, like the best person at the counter. Tone example, write your own: "Everything for the
roof, on one invoice, delivered to the mailboat."

## 10. Conversion
Primary: add to basket and continue to their real checkout (or order on WhatsApp). Secondary: call the store,
contractor pricing.

## 11. SEO
`Store` or `HomeAndConstructionBusiness` with `Product` and `Offer` only where real prices exist, `department`
for branches. Queries: "<product> Nassau", "building supplies Bahamas", "office supplies Nassau".

## 12. References
aesop.com (product stills, calm catalogue), hay.com (catalogue that reads like a magazine), muji.com (practical
calm), byredo.com (product as hero).

## 13. Traps
A brochure for a real shop; invented products or prices; a fake payment flow; a cluttered marketplace look;
gimmicks (tape, stickers); empty side columns.

## 14. Done looks like
A Family Island customer finds the product, understands delivery to their island and gets to checkout in
under a minute, and the owner sees a store better than their own.
