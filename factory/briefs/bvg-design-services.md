## Current site audit (owner-facing)
1. Footer says "Last Updated on 30 May, 2014": a web design firm whose own site stopped in 2014 loses the client at first glance.
2. The advertising agency page link is dead (404).
3. Text set entirely in bold, GIF buttons, no mobile layout: it reads badly on a phone, where most Bahamian clients now look.
4. Two phone numbers in public (602-0658 on the site, 373-2268 on Yabsta) and two emails (info@ and mail@bahamasvg.com): clients don't know which one works.
5. Published prices from 2014 (hosting, submissions) still show, which can anchor a client to old rates.
6. Your best proof, 30 years and the Bahamas Vacation Guide, is buried in a jokey paragraph.

## Audience
Bahamian small and mid businesses, tourism operators (hotels, charters, rentals, restaurants) on Grand Bahama, Nassau and Abaco, plus overseas owners promoting Bahamian products. Reached through referrals and search; they check on a phone, want to know cost, speed, and that a local person answers.

## Primary conversion
Email project request to info@bvgdesign.com (mailto prefilled from a tabbed brief builder). Secondary: call Freeport (242) 602-0658.

## Positioning
For Bahamian businesses that need to be found online, BVG Design Services is the Bahamian-owned web studio in Freeport that has been designing, hosting and getting Bahamian sites found since 1996, starting with its own Bahamas Vacation Guide.

## Strategy
- Sitemap: index.html, services.html.
- Primary CTA: "Start a website request". Secondary: "Call the Freeport office".
- Journey: arrive from search "web design Freeport Bahamas" / referral → see 1996 roots, offices, real services → fill the brief builder, email opens.
- SEO primary: "web design Freeport Grand Bahama"; secondary: "Bahamas web design", "website hosting Bahamas", "SEO Bahamas", "advertising agency Freeport".

## Buyer portrait
Owner of a 2-30 person Bahamian business (guesthouse in Lucaya, charter in Abaco, retailer in Nassau), income from tourism or local trade, budget-conscious, burned before by an overseas agency that disappeared. Compares BVG with a nephew who "does websites", Wix, and Nassau agencies. Fears: paying and never being found on Google, nobody local to call. Feels they chose well when a Bahamian firm with decades of track record answers the phone and shows a clear process.

## Brand truth / feel
Brand truth: they were getting Bahamian businesses found online before most of the Bahamas was online. Feel: Bahamian, experienced, plainspoken, practical, sunny.

## Design spec
- Kind: service (creative studio). Hero recipe: product/craft close-up + typographic monument: giant two-line statement left (cols 1-7), a live "browser" on the right that types a Bahamian search and assembles a site in the flag colours (the craft itself, their founding SEO skill).
- Type: Bricolage Grotesque 500/700 display (tight -0.035em), Instrument Sans 400/500/600 body.
- Palette (from the Bahamian flag, since the firm is Bahamian owned): paper #f6f5f1, paper-2 #ecebe4, ink #101515, aqua #007f88 (text-safe aquamarine, 4.9:1 on paper), aqua-soft #bfe6e4, gold #f2c230, muted #5a6161, line #d9d8d0.
- Signature visual: "This shows a browser building a Bahamian business site because the brief says BVG designs Bahamian websites and began by getting its own guide to the top of search results." Second: an era slider comparing their real 1996 guide-style page with a modern layout because the brief says they launched the Bahamas Vacation Guide in mid-1996 and still maintain it. Third: office map (Freeport HQ, Nassau, Abaco, Fort Lauderdale US line).
- Interactions: era slider (drag), office map hotspots, tabbed project brief builder that opens an email, services list rows that expand.
- Motion: hero load sequence (label, line-split headline, body, CTA, browser assembles), one scroll idea per section, hover micro-interactions everywhere, scroll progress line.
